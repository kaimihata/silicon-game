Chip City — Spike Verification Plan

Quality criteria and test cases for proving the greybox prototype matches the intended gameplay.

1. Verification principle

Do not accept the spike because it renders or because its arithmetic appears plausible. Accept it only when a player can observe the stated cause-and-effect loop: chip choices alter a manufacturing profile; the profile alters machine behavior; factory behavior alters accepted output, waste, and money; the player can understand and respond.

Blocking condition: if a consequence is only visible in a debug log, hidden formula, or unexplained total score, the spike is not ready.

5. Usability and learning test

Use at least two fresh testers who have not read the internal design documents. Do not explain the intended solution. Observe whether they can complete the following in a short session.

  1. Identify the contract’s pass requirement and why a design is not eligible.
  2. Predict what moving memory closer will do, then verify the result after moving it.
  3. Identify why the factory is not starting or why wafers are queued.
  4. Explain why a hot design makes less money even if its parts initially look cheap.
  5. State one reason to buy a second scanner and one reason not to.
Usability gatePass condition
Cause-and-effect comprehensionTester can name a design choice, the affected machine/business outcome, and a possible response without prompting.

6. Quality bar

Must be true

  • All golden scenarios are reproducible.
  • Every displayed number has a player-facing explanation.
  • Costs, yields, rates, and contract counting are internally consistent.
  • Design and factory screens show the same manufacturing profile.
  • Performance is smooth enough that flow and queues are readable.

Do not accept if

  • Players can solve the loop only by trial-and-error with no diagnosis.
  • Waste is merely a percentage and does not have a physical route.
  • Factory capacity is disconnected from chip choices.
  • Premium parts are always optimal or always useless.
  • The implementation adds campaign/visual scope before the core loop works.

7. Implementation review protocol

  1. Builder completes the spike and records which requirements were implemented or deferred.
  2. Reviewer runs the functional checklist and all five golden scenarios.
  3. Reviewer checks displayed numbers against the build brief's paper values or explicitly documented replacements.
  4. Fresh-user test is run; failures are classified as unclear feedback, broken rule, balance issue, or missing feature.
  5. Only then decide whether to iterate on the spike or expand scope.
